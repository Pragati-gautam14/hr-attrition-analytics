Key insights

  • Attrition is 16.1% (237 of 1,470 employees), an estimated $6.8M replacement cost (assuming 50% of annual salary per leaver). First-year attrition is 34.9%.
  • Overtime is the biggest controllable driver: 31% attrition with overtime vs 10% without. At job level 1 it is 53% vs 16%. Bringing overtime employees down to their level's non-overtime rate would avoid ≈83 exits (35% of leavers, ≈$2.3M).
  • Two flight-risk profiles hold 45% of leavers but only 15% of staff: (1) under 40, under 3 years' tenure, no stock options: 52% attrition; (2) entry-level staff working overtime: 46%.
  • Role hotspots: Sales Representatives (40%) and Laboratory Technicians (24%, the most leavers: 62) both have median pay below $3K a month. Employees earning under $3K a month leave at 29%.
  • Retention levers: employees with no stock options leave at 24% vs 8–9% with level 1–2 grants. Low work-life balance (score 1) means 31% attrition.
  • Pay equity check passed: women's median pay is 5% higher than men's. Controlling for job level, experience, department and education, the adjusted gap is 0.01% (p = 0.99, not significant).
